Aerodynamic Loads & Wing Performance

Status: Complete

Project Overview

Welcome to the showcase of my Aerodynamic Loads lab project. In this experiment, I investigated the aerodynamic forces on a symmetric rectangular wing in a subsonic, low-turbulence wind tunnel. The primary objective was to measure force and torque components to calculate non-dimensional coefficients—specifically Lift, Drag, and Pitching Moment. The wing was evaluated in four main configurations: with and without an end plate, and across high (~16.5 m/s) and low (~12.5 m/s) speed environments at varying angles of attack.

Testing Setup & Methodology

The experiment was conducted in an open-return Low Turbulence Wind Tunnel, carefully instrumented to guarantee accurate and repeatable measurements. The physical setup included:

  • An ATI Gamma SI-130-10 six-component load cell mounted on the wing's quarter-chord line to record all three force and moment components.
  • A Pitot-static probe connected to a Baratron capacitance-type pressure transducer, used to measure dynamic pressure as a voltage change.
  • A stepper motor and ESP32 microcontroller system that precisely rotated the wing through angles of attack from 0° to 22°.
  • A FlowKinetics 3DP1A Manometer to record ambient room pressure and temperature for air density calculations.

Data Reduction & Mathematical Modeling

Once raw voltage and load cell data were collected (logging 1000 samples per second), several formulas were implemented via a MATLAB script to reduce the data into meaningful aerodynamic characteristics.

  • Dynamic Pressure & Airspeed: Dynamic pressure was calculated using q∞ = Baratron Voltage × 135.455 Pa/Volt. True airspeed was then derived using v∞ = √(2q∞ / ρ).
  • Aerodynamic Forces: Lift and drag forces were derived by resolving the x- and y- force components relative to the angle of attack (α): Lift (L = Fx cos(α) - Fy sin(α)) and Drag (D = Fy cos(α) + Fx sin(α)).
  • Non-Dimensional Coefficients: The coefficient of lift was calculated as CL = L / (q∞S), where S represents the planform area. The moment coefficient was similarly calculated using CM = M / (q∞Sc).

Key Findings & Results

The reduced data revealed significant insights into how flow speed and physical wing barriers affect aerodynamic efficiency.

  • Both lift and drag coefficients exhibited an increasing linear relationship with the angle of attack until the wing reached its critical stall point.
  • Upon stalling, all wing configurations experienced a dramatic drop in lift, a sudden spike in drag, and a sharp positive reversal in the moment coefficient.
  • Higher freestream velocity proved to be the most significant factor in delaying flow separation, raising the critical stall angle from approximately 12° to 19°.
  • The inclusion of the end plate physically blocked flow around the span, effectively suppressing tip vortices, reducing induced drag, and producing a higher maximum lift coefficient.
  • Error Analysis: While random error was minimized through high-frequency sampling repetition, unavoidable systematic error from the load cell (+/- 0.05 N) and Baratron dominated the overall measurement uncertainty.
