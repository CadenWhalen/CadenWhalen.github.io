ME 1670 Mini Ant Robot for SAR

Status: Complete

Project Overview

Welcome to the showcase of my ME 1670 Capstone Project. In this project, I collaborated with Rayyan Rahman and Luke Tumpalan under the instruction of Dr. Raghu Pucha to design, develop, and implement a Mini Ant Robot for search-and-rescue (SAR) missions. Below is a detailed overview of the skills gained as well as the project's conceptualization, design process, development, technical challenges, progress, and final implementation.

The Main Idea

Natural disasters inevitably take place around the world, and certain buildings cannot withstand the impact. When these buildings collapse, many humans may become trapped with little hope for escape, and our Mini Ant Robot was designed to locate and aid in the rescue of these victims. For movement, we took inspiration from small creatures like spiders and worms, incorporating small spider legs in the front and rear alongside side treads.

The project consisted of key geometric subassemblies and functional requirements. My primary personal responsibilities included the Camera Subassembly and the Battery Subassembly. The camera subassembly was responsible for relaying visual information using two servo motors for a 360-degree view. The battery subassembly featured a quick and simple push-push mechanism allowing users to easily replace batteries on the move. Rayyan Rahman managed the suspension and treads subassembly, and Luke Tumpalan handled the spider leg subassembly.

Project Progress

Throughout the Fall 2025 semester, our team successfully advanced from initial hand sketches to detailed SolidWorks modeling, manufacturing working drawings, and comprehensive subassembly instructions. We successfully integrated the frame, camera, battery compartment, drivetrain, suspension, and telescoping spider legs into a cohesive final assembly.

Functionality checks confirmed that the lower camera servo provides full horizontal 360-degree motion while the upper servo has a limited range to protect against frame collisions. Furthermore, the push-push battery mechanism functions reliably via initial and secondary spring compressions, and the mechanical drivetrain and spider legs successfully execute multi-axis motion for rough terrain traversal.

Successes and Challenges

The development of the Mini Ant Robot involved notable milestones and engineering hurdles:

Successes

  • Successfully designed and modeled a fully functional 360-degree camera tracking system
  • Created a seamless push-push hot-swappable battery mechanism
  • Successfully integrated mechanical suspension, tank treads, and articulating spider legs
  • Produced detailed SolidWorks manufacturing drawings and assembly documentation

Challenges

  • Dramatically altering the robot's frame to reduce material usage and ensure proper fit
  • Increasing the size of the robot's treads to maintain stability across uneven terrain
  • Coordinating multi-member subassembly dimensions and mates prior to final integration

Engineering Skills Gained

Working on this capstone project allowed me to expand my engineering capabilities significantly outside the classroom. The main visualization and design skills developed include:

  • Engineering graphics and visualization
  • Advanced SolidWorks part and assembly modeling
  • Creation of manufacturing working drawings and bills of materials
  • Mechanism design including servo integration and spring-loaded push mechanisms
  • Collaborative engineering design and cross-subassembly communication

Final Thoughts & Conclusion

The main goals of our Mini Ant Robot for SAR were fully met with our final design. The robot is capable of recording and relaying information to its user while maintaining robust vertical and horizontal mobility across rough terrain. This project provided an invaluable hands-on experience in mechanical design and visualization.
